### Canon labels

- **Confirmed canon:** Objectively true within the setting unless deliberately revised later.
- **Public knowledge:** What an ordinary resident could reasonably know.
- **Professional knowledge:** What Wardens, engineers, Archivists, or Threadbearers may know.
- **Scholarly theory:** A reasoned in-world interpretation that may be incomplete.
- **Disputed history:** Accounts conflict or were distorted by political interests.
- **Open design:** Intentionally unresolved and not yet safe to treat as fact.
- **Superseded:** An earlier idea retained only for development history.

## 1. Creative north star

Brassreach is a dwarven city built through a union of craft, public stewardship, natural forces, and living resonance. Its founders learned to join dissonant sounds into harmonies so complete that they could shape water, stone, metal, and growth. Centuries of this work gave rise to an emergent life beneath the city: the Unfathomer.

The founders never knew what they had made. The Unfathomer rested peacefully within the harmonious environment that had brought it into being. As Brassreach became wealthy, hierarchical, and neglectful, social failure became material failure: cheaper alloys, abandoned channels, exhausted workers, contradictory civic instructions, and whole districts denied proper care. The city's great resonant network fell into discord.

The Unfathomer is not attacking Brassreach. It is suffering within it. Unable to reason or speak as a person would, it spreads upward through resonance and water in a blind search for the harmony it remembers as home. Its movement raises the cisterns beyond the limits of decaying infrastructure.

The player begins as a probationary Threadbearer—a public investigator, cartographer, witness, and mediator. By repairing local failures, preserving testimony, rebuilding cooperation, and following one recurring disturbance into the under-works, the player creates the first connected improvement the Unfathomer has felt in generations. At the Gate of Measures, the player cannot repair centuries of decline in a night. They can prove that change has begun, calm the Unfathomer, and win Brassreach the time and political opportunity to become worthy of its inheritance again.

The story is therefore not about defeating a monster. It is about discovering that a civic crisis, an ecological crisis, and a magical crisis are the same wound viewed from different levels.

## 2. Core themes

### 2.1 Stewardship over possession

The natural world and the works that sustain public life are responsibilities, not prizes. Early Brassreach understood this. Later Brassreach increasingly treated land, water, labor, and inherited craft as resources to exploit.

### 2.2 Moral choices become material conditions

The setting must not imply that greed creates supernatural “bad vibrations” merely because greed is immoral. The causal chain is concrete:

```text
Unequal values
→ unequal laws and budgets
→ neglected workers and districts
→ poor materials and contradictory repairs
→ physical and resonant discord
→ the Unfathomer's distress and expansion
→ flooding and structural crisis
```

The blurred line between magic and technology makes these consequences deeper, but it does not replace cause and effect.

### 2.3 Truth requires a continuous record

Facts can be scattered, minimized, reclassified, or ignored. They should not be easy to erase. Threadbearers preserve the line between what was decided and what followed.

### 2.4 Repair is both physical and civic

No ending can permanently restore Brassreach through machinery alone. A stable future requires repaired infrastructure, truthful records, skilled labor, institutional cooperation, and changes in how the city treats its people and environment.

### 2.5 The unknown remains genuinely unknown

Lithen's learning does not make her omniscient. The Unfathomer cannot explain itself. The player may reach the strongest available interpretation without converting every mystery into certainty.

### 2.6 Progress is not instant redemption

The player can avert immediate catastrophe and create proof that a better direction is possible. They cannot erase centuries of injustice during one expedition. Even the best ending begins a difficult civic project rather than completing it.

## 3. Brassreach

### 3.1 The city

**Confirmed canon:** Brassreach is a large, dwarven-majority city of terraces, civic halls, workshops, archives, tuned caverns, reservoirs, and ancient under-works. Its brilliant center rises in brass and golden stone while its outer and lower districts show accumulated crowding, dust, patched construction, and neglect.

Brassreach was not built at once. Generations extended it along natural stone, watercourses, and carefully shaped resonant chambers. Its oldest works do not dominate nature through brute force. They collaborate with weight, pressure, material, and vibration.

The distinction between magic and engineering is intentionally blurred. A Brassreach craftsperson might regard a modern scholar's attempt to separate the two as evidence that the scholar does not understand either.

### 3.2 Geographic structure

Brassreach descends both socially and physically.

#### High Terraces

The wealthy center of government, hereditary houses, ceremonial halls, fine markets, and well-maintained public spaces. Its residents receive the most reliable water, lighting, and repairs. The High Terraces are beautiful, but their beauty increasingly depends upon burdens displaced elsewhere.

#### The Halls

Upper civic corridors, public offices, bell-stairs, inspection passages, map rooms, and major entrances into the older works. The Halls are where official Brassreach meets the infrastructure beneath it. Water can be heard under many floors.

The Halls are not the player's first exposure to danger, but they are the threshold where a local investigation becomes a sanctioned descent.

#### The Archives

The city's repository of laws, maintenance histories, testimony, engineering diagrams, civic registers, and Threadbearer records. It includes public reading rooms, restricted stacks, resonant record wells, restoration workshops, and old indexing mechanisms.

The Archives preserve truth more successfully than Brassreach acts upon it.

#### The Tangles

A dense craft and residential district of ropewalks, wire presses, small workshops, stacked dwellings, and improvised bridges. It is rich in rumor and practical knowledge but receives inconsistent Warden attention. Piera operates here.

#### The Brassworks

Foundries, bell shops, tuning floors, pressure works, machine halls, and material yards. The Brassworks are essential to the city's public systems but politically subordinate to the High Houses that fund and purchase their output.

#### Outer and Lower Wards

Crowded districts beyond the best-maintained center. These wards bear the earliest effects of failed drainage, structural shifts, and interrupted services. Their residents are not a culturally uniform faction; they are united primarily by being asked to endure problems the High Terraces can postpone.

#### The Depths

Warden posts, cistern access routes, sluice catwalks, pressure galleries, buried workshops, vaults, and ancient service tunnels. The Depths are dangerous because they combine active infrastructure with centuries of additions whose records do not always agree.

#### The Cistern Fields

Immense natural and shaped caverns holding reservoirs, waterways, resonant stone, and the oldest surviving works. The boundary between civic infrastructure and the Unfathomer's body becomes increasingly difficult to define here.

#### The Gate of Measures

A monumental circular structure in a vast underground atrium. It is made from dark stone veined with brass, marked by moss, water, and age. At roughly one hundred feet or more in height, it does not resemble a door that simply swings open.

The Gate is simultaneously a calibration mechanism, a constitutional memorial, a teaching instrument, and a means of reaching the deepest resonant network.

#### Porkkala

An upland spur and quarry district outside the central terraces. Early bell-metal and resonant stone were worked there. Its abandoned cuts and surviving tuning chambers may provide a refuge or alternate channel in one ending.

### 3.3 Population and outsiders

Brassreach is dwarven-majority and culturally dwarven, but trade and skilled migration have brought humans, elves, gnomes, halflings, orcs, and others into the city.

A non-dwarven Threadbearer is unusual, not impossible. Their appointment may attract curiosity or prejudice. Their distance from inherited dwarven assumptions can also help them notice conditions residents have normalized.

A dwarven player may likewise begin outside the city's privileged center: from an outer ward, migrant branch, works family, or other background with limited access to the High Houses.

## 4. Historical framework

Exact dates and calendar names remain **open design**. The following ages are canonical as a relative chronology.

### 4.1 The Founding

The founders settled around natural caverns, watercourses, and stone unusually responsive to vibration. They developed resonance craft by listening before shaping. Early works were built slowly, with public maintenance and environmental stewardship treated as inseparable.

The principles later summarized as Weight, Tone, Pattern, and living Choice emerged from practical construction rather than abstract philosophy.

### 4.2 The Great Making

Brassreach expanded. Bells regulated water. Tuned chambers distributed pressure. Resonant tools shaped stone and supported living systems. Repeated harmonies passed through the under-works for generations.

During this age, living resonance accumulated and the Unfathomer gradually came into being. No known founder recognized it as a discrete life. The stable harmony of the city kept it peaceful and nearly dormant.

### 4.3 The Ascendancy of the Houses

Successful families converted prestige, stewardship, and commercial influence into hereditary authority. Temporary leadership became permanent seats. Shared resources remained public in name while decisions about them moved upward.

The Founding Covenant was not abolished. Its language was narrowed through exceptions, amendments, restricted interpretation, and unequal enforcement.

### 4.4 The Long Dissonance

Brassreach pursued material wealth while losing respect for the conditions that had made its wealth possible. Craft became production. Maintenance became expense. Workers lost political standing. Old systems were patched with incompatible materials. Records accumulated warnings that were acknowledged but not resolved.

The Unfathomer began to experience this spreading discord throughout its continuous body. Its first movements were too subtle to distinguish from ordinary settling and water fluctuation.

### 4.5 The Rising Years

For several years before the game, unexplained flooding, reverse pressure, resonant faults, and synchronized failures grew more frequent. Each incident was treated locally. Public explanations blamed weather, old pipes, poor workers, or isolated mechanical faults.

Professionals in the Depths understood that the incidents were connected before they understood how. Lithen suspected an unprecedented form of living resonance but refused to name or publicize it without stronger evidence.

### 4.6 The present crisis

Water is now rising beyond the city's decaying capacity. The entity beneath Brassreach is searching outward for the harmonies that once surrounded it. The public does not know the Unfathomer exists. Most citizens know only that repairs fail too quickly and strange patterns are appearing in unrelated parts of the city.

The player's story begins before the crisis has an accepted name.

## 5. Living resonance and the Unfathomer

### 5.1 Living resonance

**Confirmed canon:** Perfectly integrated resonance can do more than transmit energy. Under rare and sustained conditions, it can carry organization, responsiveness, memory-like continuity, and ultimately life.

“Living resonance” is the accepted scholarly term for this category. It should not be explained as ordinary sound becoming a ghost. It is a form of magical-technological life arising from relationships among vibration, matter, water, repeated craft, and intention.

Smaller living resonances may exist in old Brassreach works, but none approach the scale or continuity of the Unfathomer. Their existence is a future story opportunity, not yet a requirement.

### 5.2 Lithen's name

**Confirmed canon:** Lithen the Wise coins the term **Unfathomer** after the player reaches the Archives and provides evidence connecting multiple disturbances.

She chooses the name because the phenomenon cannot be completely measured, bounded, or understood—not even, perhaps, by itself. Lithen does not publish the name before she believes the concept is responsible enough to enter public language.

Before that moment:

- Ordinary citizens have no concept of the Unfathomer.
- Public speech concerns bad water, settling faults, failed bells, or strange pressure.
- Workers may have local superstitions, but none are confirmed descriptions of the entity.
- The game's opening must not reveal the term to the player as common knowledge.

### 5.3 Body and consciousness

The Unfathomer is a massive, continuous, borderline-intangible entity whose organization is carried through resonance interacting with water, stone, and metal.

It is not literally made only of water. Water is its most visible medium and the part of its movement most dangerous to Brassreach. Its awareness is distributed across its full extent with near-instant agreement, analogous to a single enormous nervous system.

It is neither a conventional individual nor a chorus of separate minds.

### 5.4 Awareness and limits

The Unfathomer can perceive:

- Harmonic compatibility and dissonance.
- The direction and intensity of resonant change.
- Patterns repeated through the systems it inhabits.
- Intention when intention is expressed through consequential action and resonant craft.
- Familiar qualities associated with the harmony in which it first rested.

It cannot reliably:

- Use complex spoken language.
- Explain its origin.
- Understand civic institutions as people understand them.
- Track individual human or dwarven lives as morally distinct units.
- Formulate a conventional political bargain.
- Lie, scheme, taunt, or deliver riddles.

### 5.5 Motivation

The Unfathomer seeks relief from pervasive discord. Its expansion resembles a root system, neural web, or bacterial growth searching for conditions that permit it to settle.

It does not seek nutrition, conquest, or revenge. It searches for the resonant “home” that once surrounded it.

### 5.6 Why the water rises

As the Unfathomer expands, it couples previously separate reservoirs and resonant channels into increasingly synchronized pressure systems. Water follows the spreading field, levels rise together, and local controls lose their ability to isolate surges.

Centuries-old infrastructure cannot bear the resulting pressure. This creates flooding, structural damage, displaced animals, and dangerous changes in old mechanisms.

### 5.7 Communication

The Unfathomer does not speak in sentences. Communication occurs through response:

- Pressure easing or increasing.
- Water changing direction.
- Light and suspended particles arranging into patterns.
- Old sounds returning in altered form.
- Shared sensory impressions.
- Emotional qualities such as distress, recognition, hesitation, or relief.
- Resonant mechanisms accepting or rejecting a configuration.

At the Gate, these responses become clearer because the Gate amplifies and organizes intention into forms the Unfathomer can perceive. Lithen may interpret what happens, but she must distinguish observation from theory.

### 5.8 Why the player's actions matter

The player does not heal the city with one decision.

On the journey downward, they create a connected chain of real improvements:

- A public mechanism is repaired correctly rather than cheaply patched.
- Neglected testimony enters the record.
- Workers and officials cooperate across institutional boundaries.
- A damaged resonant node is returned to its intended relation with neighboring systems.
- Each Key restores one part of the old network.
- The route itself becomes a sequence of functioning harmonic anchors.

For the first time in generations, the Unfathomer feels change moving consistently toward harmony rather than away from it.

At the Gate, the network connects those repairs and carries the player's demonstrated intention into the deepest cisterns. The entity perceives not a promise made in words, but an established pattern and the possibility that it will continue. Its distress eases enough to halt or slow its expansion.

Brassreach is thereby granted genuine time. Whether it uses that time wisely becomes the central question of the epilogue and future stories.

## 6. The Founding Measures

### 6.1 Status

The Measures are not confirmed universal laws of reality. They are the founders' practical framework for creating works that remain sound across time.

Brassreach often describes them as **three Measures and the living Choice**. The first three can be represented by tools and Keys. The last requires an accountable living person.

### 6.2 Weight — Stone

Weight asks what a work must bear and who will bear its cost.

It governs foundations, pressure, load, endurance, protection, and material consequence. In civic terms, it exposes when comfort in one district is purchased by danger in another.

### 6.3 Tone — Brass

Tone asks whether separate parts can work together without destroying one another.

It governs resonance, timing, communication, mechanical accord, and the quality of relation among systems. It is not mere musical beauty. A harmonious system remains distinct in its parts while functioning as a whole.

### 6.4 Pattern — Echo

Pattern asks what is being repeated, remembered, inherited, or concealed.

It governs recurrence, precedent, stored configuration, maintenance history, and the persistence of consequence. Pattern allows a system to remember successful states, but it can also preserve injustice when harmful choices are repeated.

### 6.5 Choice — the living bearer

Choice asks what should happen now.

No Key supplies it. No machine can absolve the living of responsibility by claiming that history, harmony, or necessity made the decision inevitable. The chooser must understand Weight, Tone, and Pattern, then accept accountability for what follows.

### 6.6 Founders' refrain

The preferred traditional verse is:

> Stone bears the load.  
> Brass carries the song.  
> Echo holds what the ages pass on.  
> Three Keys wake the old works below;  
> the living must choose where tomorrow will go.

This refrain may have regional or historical variants, but its meaning should remain stable.

### 6.7 Superseded frameworks

The following are no longer primary canon:

- Tune / Name / Measure / Decide as four cosmic disciplines.
- Weight / Tone / Pattern / Line.
- Line as a fourth Measure.
- Consent as a missing fourth Measure.

Older texts may preserve these as obsolete scholarly models, mistranslations, or development history, but player-facing lore should use Weight, Tone, Pattern, and living Choice.

## 7. The Founding Covenant and Cadence Law

### 7.1 The Founding Covenant

The Founding Covenant is Brassreach's constitution. It is an agreement among the city's people and institutions, not a pact with the Unfathomer.

Its confirmed principles are:

- Essential water and public works are held in common trust.
- Civic authority carries duties that cannot be inherited without accountability.
- The effects of a decision must be recorded alongside its stated purpose.
- Skilled labor deserves both dignity and a voice in decisions affecting the works.
- Public records may not be secretly erased.
- Future generations are parties to decisions about foundational resources.
- No machine, office, or inherited rule replaces living judgment.

The exact original clauses and legal language remain **open design**. They should be developed only when a story requires them.

### 7.2 Corruption without repeal

The High Houses never openly renounced the Covenant. They weakened it through narrow readings, exceptions, procedural barriers, hereditary appointments, selective budgets, and the separation of decisions from their consequences.

This is important: Brassreach's decline did not require every leader to become malicious. It required enough people to benefit from a system that made responsibility difficult to see.

### 7.3 Cadence Law

Cadence Law is the combined legal and engineering tradition used to keep civic decisions compatible with Brassreach's living systems.

At its broadest, a major public action should:

1. Establish what must bear the cost.
2. Test whether affected systems can work in accord.
3. consult the recorded pattern of earlier attempts.
4. Name an accountable living decision-maker.
5. Preserve the observed result for later judgment.

Cadence Law does not make every spoken sentence magical. Its force comes from the union of public authority, recorded evidence, resonant mechanisms, and repeated civic practice.

The precise boundary between legal authority and resonant effect remains intentionally blurred.

### 7.4 How stories become law

“Stories are woven into law” means that lived events become public evidence and precedent when their full thread is preserved.

A Threadbearer's account does not automatically command reality. It can:

- Establish what happened in a place officials did not witness.
- Connect a decision to later consequences.
- Contradict an incomplete official summary.
- Become binding precedent after civic review.
- Supply the factual and resonant pattern needed for a lawful citywide change.

Across generations, repeated laws and practices also shape the physical city. In Brassreach, the difference between social precedent and resonant pattern is narrower than it would be elsewhere.

## 8. Threadbearers

### 8.1 Purpose

Threadbearers are chartered civic field witnesses. They follow events through geography, testimony, machinery, and consequence, then return an account whose chain cannot be quietly broken.

Their practical identity is:

```text
investigator + cartographer + inspector + witness + mediator
```

They are not prophets, bards who rewrite reality, or agents with unlimited legal authority.

### 8.2 Duties

Threadbearers may be asked to:

- Inspect disputed or dangerous conditions.
- Map routes and public hazards.
- Gather testimony from affected residents and workers.
- Compare official records with physical evidence.
- Mediate immediate conflicts when delay would cause harm.
- Make limited emergency rulings within their writ.
- Preserve discoveries in the public record.
- Identify where a civic decision's consequences diverged from its stated intent.

### 8.3 Thread Ledgers

A Thread Ledger uses resonant brass leaves, treated paper, indexing thread, and registered seals. When an entry is closed at a civic register, a corresponding pattern is impressed into Archive master plates.

The first Threadbearers preserved their field accounts by physically weaving ordered testimony with needle and thread. Different materials, knots, and stitch intervals distinguished direct witness, measured fact, disputed claim, and later correction. The practice gave the profession its name. Modern ledgers use etched brass leaves and resonant indexing, but ceremonial binding thread remains visible along their spines so the office remembers that a trustworthy account joins distinct strands without pretending they are identical.

An entry can be challenged, annotated, or politically ignored. It cannot be altered or removed without leaving a detectable discontinuity in both the field ledger and master record.

This makes secrecy difficult without making the institution incorruptible. Authorities can still overwhelm truth with delay, hostile interpretation, restricted access, or countless irrelevant records.

### 8.4 Authority and limits

Threadbearers do not legislate alone. Their authority is strongest when:

- Conditions are dangerous and ordinary review is impossible.
- Multiple civic offices dispute the same facts.
- A public work lies beyond normal oversight.
- Existing maps or records are demonstrably unreliable.

Their emergency decisions must later enter public review. Abuse cannot be perfectly prevented, but it cannot be made invisible.

### 8.5 Training and the Deep Writ

Threadbearers train at the **Threadbearer Institute**, a small civic school attached to the upper Halls and authenticated by the Archives. Its curriculum combines field surveying, structural observation, interviewing, public-record law, route finding, emergency judgment, and the disciplined separation of fact from inference. Graduates are qualified to serve but do not receive unrestricted authority.

The player begins as a newly graduated, probationary Threadbearer performing supervised work in the open Halls, surface districts, and staffed public works. Captain Brunna knows the player from practical evaluations and believes they may possess the judgment required for deeper work.

A **Deep Writ** is a special authorization allowing a Threadbearer to enter restricted under-works, inspect sealed civic systems, request records across offices, and carry Keys under witnessed custody. It is a permit, not a separate profession.

Captain Brunna oversees the player's field evaluation and grants the Deep Writ after the first joined account proves the player can connect failures without inventing their cause. Lithen later authenticates its Archive basis, authorizes restricted records, and releases the Echo Key under witnessed custody. Warden and Archive support together make the descent politically plausible without making either office the player's owner.

### 8.6 Why the player is chosen

During the tutorial, the player does more than complete assigned tasks. They notice that unrelated failures carry the same low overtone and that careful, cooperative repairs briefly reduce it.

The important qualities are:

- Attention to physical evidence.
- Willingness to hear low-status witnesses.
- Judgment under pressure.
- Ability to act without concealing failure.
- Recognition of a pattern everyone else treated as separate accidents.

This earns Brunna's Deep Writ and a provisional assignment below the public Halls. Lithen's later authentication extends the investigation into restricted Archive systems and establishes the evidentiary basis for carrying the expedition into the Depths.

### 8.7 The Weaver

**Current canon:** “The Weaver” is not an established god, office, supernatural force, or speaking in-world entity.

The term may remain as a non-diegetic name for the story engine or interface. Player-facing narrative should not attribute events to a Weaver unless that concept is deliberately designed and approved later.

## 9. Government and civic groups

### 9.1 The Council of Houses

Brassreach is presently governed by a council dominated by hereditary High Houses. The precise number and names of the Houses remain **open design**.

The Council controls major budgets, appointments, trade policy, and long-term public works. Civic offices retain formal independence but depend upon Council funding and access.

The Council is not a secret cult or unified villain. Its members have competing interests. Some sincerely believe they preserve stability. Others knowingly protect privilege. Together they sustain a structure that separates decisions from those who bear their consequences.

### 9.2 The High Houses

The Houses descend from families who transformed early civic responsibility into inherited authority. Their wealth is concentrated in trade, land use, foundries, finance, and access to the best-maintained districts.

House culture should not be portrayed as uniformly decadent. Individual members may become allies, critics, reformers, or obstacles. The institution remains the problem even when an individual behaves honorably.

### 9.3 The Works Comptroller

The **Works Comptroller** is an office responsible for approving large maintenance expenditures and reconciling requests among districts. It provides a useful narrative chain through the campaign:

- A surface repair was delayed or reduced by the office.
- Archive records reveal a recurring pattern of rejected warnings.
- Depths crews can identify the practical consequences.
- The player's final report makes the pattern publicly undeniable.

The current Works Comptroller is **Halvek**. His House affiliation remains open design. He embodies institutional rationalization rather than serving as the sole cause of the crisis: defensive, procedural, and accustomed to treating lawful fragments as proof that no unlawful whole exists. His cooperation, exposure, or resistance can affect resources during the expedition and the strength of the epilogue.

### 9.4 Civic Wardens

Wardens handle public safety, structural emergencies, restricted routes, evacuations, and protection of critical works. They are practical and decentralized. Their quality varies by district and command.

Wardens are not a military caste ruling the city. Their authority is strongest during immediate danger.

### 9.5 The Archives

The Archives preserve civic memory, law, technical history, and Threadbearer records. Archivists authenticate documents and help determine how precedent applies.

The institution is not guilty of fabricating the crisis. Its weakness is that preserving a warning does not ensure anyone acts upon it. Restrictions, classification systems, and professional caution can make true information difficult to use.

### 9.6 Worksfolk

Worksfolk include cistern crews, brassworkers, bellfounders, builders, haulers, pressure engineers, rope crews, and repair laborers. They are indispensable to Brassreach and occupy a lower political tier than their importance warrants.

Their knowledge is often practical, local, and transmitted through apprenticeship. Official reports frequently reduce that knowledge to costs or work orders.

### 9.7 The Mullinen Wardens

The Mullinen Wardens are a public-works and emergency tradition descended from the followers, family, and students of Mullinen the Stout.

They combine physical defense with maintenance of the deepest works. Over time, Mullinen's ethic of protecting workers through prepared endurance was distorted into a harsher belief that workers prove their worth by accepting sacrifice.

The order is internally divided between those interpretations.

### 9.8 The Brass Choir

The Brass Choir is a guild and teaching tradition of bellfounders, tuners, and resonant engineers. Its masters can loosen, tighten, diagnose, or align mechanisms through controlled tones, though stories exaggerate this into “singing bolts from the wall.”

The Choir preserves part of the founders' resonance craft. It does not understand living resonance as completely as Lithen eventually theorizes. Its institutional custody of the Brass Key gives it a central role in the main campaign.

### 9.9 Threadbearers

Threadbearers remain a small chartered profession positioned among offices rather than beneath one. Their practical independence is never perfect; it depends upon public trust, Archive authentication, and the willingness of Wardens to honor a valid writ.

## 10. Named people and institutions

### 10.1 Lithen the Wise

**Status:** Confirmed canon  
**Role:** Chief bookkeeper and librarian of the Archives

Lithen is an elderly, deeply learned individual with a disciplined approach to language. She prefers an incomplete truth clearly labeled over a confident invention.

She serves as the story's principal expositional guide, but exposition should arise from evidence the player helps uncover. She does not know the Unfathomer's full origin at first. She develops and refines her theory as records, measurements, and observations accumulate.

Lithen coins **Unfathomer** only after the player's evidence demonstrates that the disturbances behave like one continuous phenomenon. The name should first appear in her presence.

She is kind without being soft-minded, authoritative without claiming certainty, and patient with honest confusion. She should never bury meaning beneath decorative riddles.

Her central desire is to make the city's accumulated knowledge usable before it becomes an archive of preventable losses.

### 10.2 Mullinen the Stout

**Status:** Confirmed historical canon  
**Role:** Founding-era engineer, organizer, and defender of public works

Mullinen was a woman remembered for helping transform early Brassreach from a dangerous settlement into a durable city. She believed endurance was meaningful only when used to protect life and preserve the works entrusted to a community.

Later traditions simplified her into a symbol of stoic sacrifice. Her image is now invoked both by workers demanding safe conditions and commanders demanding that workers endure unsafe ones.

The story may uncover her actual writings or recorded decisions. These should reveal practical compassion, not abstract sainthood.

### 10.3 Orra Vale

**Status:** Confirmed canon  
**Role:** Commander of a Mullinen watch in the Depths

Orra is responsible for a specific group holding endangered cistern routes. She is competent, exhausted, and accustomed to choosing among inadequate options.

She begins with a partly distorted understanding of Mullinen's legacy: duty means holding the works regardless of personal cost. Through the campaign, she may recover the older principle that the works exist to protect people, not consume them.

Orra is not the thief of a magical record and does not need to surrender her command badge to satisfy a symbolic scale. Her trust is earned through decisions involving workers, repairs, and real physical consequences.

### 10.4 Captain Brunna

**Status:** Confirmed canon  
**Role:** Warden captain responsible for the player's field evaluation and access to the Halls

Brunna supervises practical probationary assignments near the surface. She notices when the player connects several failures that had been processed as separate incidents.

Brunna can recommend a Deep Writ and commit Warden resources, but she cannot grant every necessary authority alone. Her continued support strengthens the player's ability to coordinate evacuations and later reforms.

### 10.5 Quartermaster Dorrin

**Status:** Supporting canon  
**Role:** Warden quartermaster

Dorrin issues necessary field gear, sells additional supplies, tracks losses, and embodies the material constraints of a strained civic service. He values preparedness and dislikes romantic accounts of dangerous work.

### 10.6 Sella Flintwake

**Status:** Supporting canon  
**Role:** Salvager, former Brass Choir trainee, and mobile supplier in the lower works

Sella recovers usable material from abandoned or flooded routes and preserves practical tuning knowledge after failed budgets scattered several Choir crews. She provides trade, local knowledge, and evidence of how much infrastructure Brassreach has quietly surrendered. At the Silent Brassworks, she coordinates Choir tuners, furnace workers, pump crews, and salvagers into a shared repair.

Her work should raise practical questions about ownership, salvage, and survival without turning every purchase into a moral lecture.

### 10.7 Piera of the Tangles

**Status:** Supporting canon  
**Role:** Rumor broker and informal map trader

Piera deals in “almost true” maps assembled from delivery routes, worker gossip, old property lines, and observation. She is useful during the tutorial or early Halls investigation because official maps omit lived conditions.

Piera's information may be incomplete, but it should not be random nonsense. A clever player can identify which part of an “almost true” claim is useful.

### 10.8 The current Works Comptroller

**Status:** Open design

A named Comptroller may provide continuity from surface-level denied repairs to the final public reckoning. If used, the character must affect the conclusion through resources, testimony, Council action, or resistance. Do not add the official merely to give the player someone to dislike.

### 10.9 The Counter

**Status:** Reinterpreted supporting canon  
**Role:** A non-sentient instrument within the Gate

The Counter totals load, tonal alignment, historical pattern, and recorded responsibility. It preserves earlier Gate configurations and exposes missing inputs.

It does not speak, issue moral judgments, or possess an independent personality. The saying “the Gate forgets, but the Counter remembers” refers to its durable mechanical record.

## 11. The Three Keys

### 11.1 Origin and purpose

The Keys were made during the Great Making as portable calibration instruments for the Gate and connected city systems. They are not merely shaped pieces of metal. Each holds a stable relationship that ordinary mechanisms can reference.

Their custody was divided among institutions so that no single office could make a citywide change without cooperation.

### 11.2 Stone Key — Weight

The Stone Key calibrates foundations, pressure limits, load paths, and structural transfer. It allows the Gate to determine what the city can safely bear and where a change will place its cost.

Its custody belongs to the deep public-works tradition represented by the Mullinen Wardens.

The player earns it by demonstrating judgment during a real structural crisis and by helping Orra protect both workers and works.

### 11.3 Brass Key — Tone

The Brass Key calibrates active resonance, mechanical timing, and communication among systems. It allows the Gate to transmit a coherent adjustment without producing destructive interference.

Its custody belongs to the Brass Choir and Brassworks tradition.

The player earns it by restoring a damaged tuning work and securing cooperation among specialists whose knowledge has been undervalued.

### 11.4 Echo Key — Pattern

The Echo Key retrieves and compares stored resonant configurations. It allows the Gate to recall how the oldest network functioned and identify harmful repetition.

Its custody belongs to the Archives.

The player earns it by reconstructing the historical record honestly, including evidence inconvenient to every faction.

### 11.5 Choice has no Key

The fourth input is living judgment. The player cannot discover an object that makes the final decision for them.

The Gate can show loads, harmonies, precedent, and predicted consequences. It cannot declare which future is just.

### 11.6 Why all three matter

All three Keys should be clearly signposted and meaningfully obtainable during a normal run.

Two Keys permit emergency activation, ensuring that a missed branch does not make the campaign unwinnable. The absent Key creates a specific limitation:

- **No Stone:** The intervention cannot distribute pressure safely. Structural damage and displacement increase.
- **No Brass:** The intervention is harsh, unstable, or painful to the living resonance. Concord is impossible.
- **No Echo:** The intervention cannot fully compare the present with the founders' successful state. Brassreach risks repeating an old failure.

Three Keys enable the complete suite of endings and the strongest form of Concord.

### 11.7 Custody and persistence

Keys are not consumed by ordinary use. A major calibration leaves detectable wear or a new recorded pattern, ensuring later generations can know they were used.

Keys should never be granted randomly, found without context, sold by merchants, or treated as ordinary inventory loot.

## 12. The Gate of Measures

### 12.1 Physical canon

The Gate stands in a vast, dark, circular atrium deep within the cistern fields. It is an immense stone disc or aperture, roughly one hundred feet or more in height, crossed by brass channels like veins.

Moss is its principal visible blemish. Water, stalactites, carved stone, and deep darkness establish its scale. It does not visibly possess hinges suited to its mass.

Three Key receptacles are legible without making the whole mechanism easy to understand.

### 12.2 Functions

The Gate has four connected functions.

#### Calibration mechanism

It can read and adjust the citywide resonant network through the Three Keys and a living decision.

#### Constitutional chamber

It embodies the Founding Covenant's division of responsibility. Structural knowledge, technical harmony, historical memory, and accountable choice must come together before a foundational change can be made.

The Gate is constitutional because its architecture prevents one ruler, guild, archive, or machine from acting alone—not because it houses a magical courtroom.

#### Instruction for the future

The Gate is a material teaching system: a dwarven Rosetta stone preserving how the founders thought.

Its layers demonstrate relationships among load, resonance, stored pattern, and decision. A later civilization that had lost the founders' language could still learn by operating its weights, channels, echoes, and visible consequences.

It is therefore both a machine and a message sent forward in time.

#### Passage into the deepest network

Activation exposes routes, channels, or perceptual access to the oldest cisterns where the Unfathomer is most coherent. The Gate need not swing open like a door. Rings may separate, water may withdraw, or aligned resonance may reveal a passage that was physically inaccessible before.

### 12.3 Why it has not been opened

The Gate is extremely difficult to reach, dangerous to approach, and harder to operate. Its Keys are institutionally separated. Modern records are incomplete, the route has deteriorated, and no one office has both the authority and knowledge needed to activate it safely.

The present crisis is the first event in generations severe enough to compel cooperation.

### 12.4 The Counter

The Counter is integrated into the Gate. It compares Key inputs, visible loads, recovered patterns, and the Threadbearer's recorded journey. In interface terms, it can make the player's preparation legible without turning the climax into unexplained metaphysics.

### 12.5 The Gate and the Unfathomer

The Gate was not built to imprison or command the Unfathomer. It predates any confirmed awareness of that entity.

It nevertheless provides the best means to reach the Unfathomer because both arise from the same mature resonant network. The Gate can organize an intention into a coherent city-scale change the Unfathomer is capable of perceiving.

## 13. Campaign framework

### 13.1 Experience target

The initial complete campaign should support approximately 20–40 minutes depending on exploration and choices. It may use longer narrative passages at discoveries and act transitions, balanced by concise action scenes and clear options.

The path is mostly linear while allowing local choices, optional aid, equipment use, alternative checks, and different ending quality.

### 13.2 Knowledge ladder

The player should learn the central truth in stages.

| Stage | What the player understands |
|---|---|
| Opening | Brassreach has recurring maintenance failures and unusual water behavior. |
| Tutorial | Unrelated systems share the same low overtone; careful repairs briefly calm it. |
| Halls | The pattern extends below the mapped surface works. The failures are connected. |
| Archives | Lithen proposes one continuous living resonance and coins “Unfathomer.” |
| Archive investigation | The city's decline created a long, documented progression toward discord. |
| Depths | The phenomenon is spreading through water and resonant infrastructure, not attacking strategic targets. |
| Three Keys | Brassreach's oldest systems can transmit a coherent correction if institutions cooperate. |
| Gate | The Unfathomer is searching for remembered harmony; the player must choose what change the city can honestly begin. |
| Epilogue | The immediate crisis is altered, but Brassreach's long repair has only begun. |

### 13.3 Prologue — First Threads

The game begins above the deep Halls. The Unfathomer, Gate, and Keys are not introduced.

The player completes a selection of supervised probationary tasks:

1. Inspect unexplained water entering a lower residence or workshop.
2. Repair or diagnose a warning bell sounding at the wrong pressure.
3. Cross a damaged walkway or rescue someone during a minor failure.
4. Mediate a dispute between a works crew and an official enforcing an inadequate work order.
5. Record what occurred in a Thread Ledger and seal the entry at a civic register.

Across these tasks, one concrete anomaly recurs: the same low overtone appears in systems that plans show are mechanically separate. It may be heard in a bell, felt through standing water, and impressed faintly into the Ledger's resonant plate.

When a repair uses proper material, worker knowledge, and cooperative action, the overtone briefly settles. The player recognizes the relationship.

Brunna determines that the discovery exceeds a probationary assignment and brings the player into the Halls investigation.

### 13.4 Act I — Beneath the Halls

The player receives provisional authority, meets Dorrin, gathers field equipment, and follows the recurring overtone through service passages.

Encounters should combine:

- A broken floodgate or pressure barrier.
- Residents or Wardens endangered by deferred repairs.
- Animals displaced from rising channels, such as salt-hounds.
- An old route whose official map no longer matches the structure.
- Piera's practical but unofficial information.

The player earns the Deep Writ by producing a record that connects these incidents without overstating what caused them.

The act ends when the trace leads toward Archive systems and records older than the modern maps.

### 13.5 Act II — Lithen's hypothesis

The player reaches the Archives and meets Lithen. She does not deliver a finished answer immediately.

The player provides observations. Lithen compares them with:

- Old pressure records.
- Repeated maintenance warnings.
- Early descriptions of responsive resonance.
- Unexplained similarities across distant cistern systems.
- Changes that followed incompatible repairs.

Only after this comparison does she propose that the phenomenon behaves like one continuous, emergent life and name it the Unfathomer.

This should be one of the campaign's longer explanatory sequences. It must explain clearly which facts support her interpretation and which parts remain theory.

### 13.6 Act III — The First Register

The Archive mystery is not a stolen magical clause. The player reconstructs how Brassreach lost sight of its own foundations.

The **First Register** is the earliest surviving integrated account of the city's works and civic principles. It is incomplete as a single object because later offices separated its legal, structural, tonal, and maintenance sections into different collections.

The player discovers:

- Warnings were preserved but excluded from summaries.
- House-funded amendments permitted cheaper incompatible repairs.
- Lower-district testimony was entered but rarely cited.
- Original maps treated now-separated systems as one network.
- The Gate was meant to teach future operators how to reconstruct the whole.

The Index Guardian may remain as an old safety mechanism, but it should not speak in philosophical riddles. It verifies authorization, Key custody, and record integrity through mechanical tests.

By restoring an honest reconstruction—even where it implicates Archives, Wardens, Houses, and works traditions differently—the player earns Lithen's trust and the Echo Key.

### 13.7 Act IV — The Mullinen Depths

The player descends with records and the Echo Key to compare historical reconstruction with physical reality.

Orra Vale's watch is managing several simultaneous failures. The player's choices determine whether they merely gain passage or establish genuine cooperation.

Central beats may include:

- Reinforcing a breach without abandoning trapped workers.
- Crossing a pressure channel using equipment and local knowledge.
- Calming or diverting animals driven upward by water.
- Repairing a system whose official instructions contradict the experience of its crew.
- Discovering that the disturbance moves toward surviving harmonious nodes rather than toward population centers.

The Stone Key is held within a deep calibration shrine or Warden vault. It is released through a practical test of Weight: the player must demonstrate that they understand both structural load and human cost.

Orra's arc changes according to the player's conduct. In a strong route, she recognizes that protecting her people is the truest expression of Mullinen's legacy.

### 13.8 Act V — The Silent Brassworks

The Brass Key route is strongly encouraged and always reachable, though bypassing it remains possible under pressure.

The works have fallen silent or dangerous because generations of incompatible repairs produced destructive interference. Recovering the Key requires more than tuning one dial. The player must combine:

- Brass Choir knowledge.
- Worker testimony.
- Recovered Archive patterns.
- Physical repairs or replacement parts.
- A decision about time and risk as water continues rising.

When the works return to a stable tone, the Unfathomer's surrounding pressure noticeably eases. This is the clearest pre-Gate proof that restoration, rather than force, can affect it.

The player receives the Brass Key because the Choir recognizes that the Gate now has a plausible operator and because keeping the Key isolated has become more dangerous than using it.

### 13.9 Act VI — The Gate of Measures

Lithen, Orra, Warden support, and Brass Choir expertise converge according to the alliances the player built. They should arrive through routes appropriate to their roles rather than inexplicably bypassing every danger the player faced.

The player uses the Three Keys to read the Gate as an instructional system:

1. Stone reveals current loads and places already near collapse.
2. Brass reveals interference and the fragments of the founders' stable harmony.
3. Echo reveals earlier configurations and the historical progression into discord.
4. The Counter displays what preparation, repair, and institutional support the player actually carries.

The Gate then creates access to the deepest network.

### 13.10 Act VII — The living Choice

The final sequence replaces a conventional boss fight.

#### Weight phase

Water and structural pressure force the player to decide what will be supported during activation. Earlier rescues, equipment, Warden trust, and the Stone Key determine the cost.

#### Tone phase

The player and allies assemble a coherent signal from repaired nodes. Brassworks aid, the Brass Key, and technical discoveries determine whether the signal soothes or shocks the Unfathomer.

#### Pattern phase

The Gate replays accumulated resonant history. The player must distinguish the founders' stable relationships from later habits that merely repeated for a long time. Archive evidence and the Echo Key determine clarity.

#### Choice phase

The player chooses a course for Brassreach. The Unfathomer does not ask a spoken question. It responds to the enacted configuration and to the pattern of intention demonstrated during the journey.

The final choice itself is not nullified by one random roll. Prior checks and decisions determine whether the chosen resolution is strong, compromised, or costly.

## 14. Ending framework

### 14.1 Ending philosophy

Endings represent different responses to the same truth. They are not dialogue agreements selected from a menu and accepted by a speaking Unfathomer.

Every ending should answer:

- What happens to the immediate flood?
- What happens to the Unfathomer?
- What infrastructure survives?
- What truth becomes public?
- What obligation does Brassreach accept or avoid?
- How much time has the player bought?

The player's chosen course always occurs in some form. Preparation determines stability and cost.

### 14.2 Concord

**Concept:** Restore a coherent relationship among the city's works, public institutions, and living resonance.

**Strong requirements:** All three Keys, substantial evidence, several repaired harmonic anchors, and cooperation among Archives, Wardens, Worksfolk, and Brass Choir.

At the Gate, the player connects the repaired route into a coherent initial pattern. The Unfathomer perceives genuine relief and a sustained intention to continue. Its outward search slows and settles away from vulnerable districts.

The Founding Covenant is not magically restored to power. The player's public record exposes how far Brassreach departed from it and creates an undeniable basis for reform. The city begins a long program of repair, redistribution, and renewed stewardship.

Concord is the most complete ending, not a perfect one. Its epilogue should make clear that years of labor and political struggle remain.

**Compromised Concord:** If alliances or repairs are weak, the Unfathomer still calms, but only around a smaller network. Flood risk remains in neglected districts and reform may falter without continued pressure.

### 14.3 Channel

**Concept:** Construct a stable harmonic refuge and guide the Unfathomer's expansion toward it.

Porkkala's old quarries and tuning chambers offer one plausible destination. Their resonant stone can carry a restored founder harmony without placing the densest districts directly above it.

This does not involve a spoken bargain. The player creates a path of relief, and the Unfathomer follows it.

Channel ends the immediate rise and preserves much of Brassreach's living craft. It also allows the city to postpone deeper political reform. If the refuge is neglected or exploited, the crisis may return in another form.

### 14.4 Bind

**Concept:** Surround the deepest resonant network with dampening works and isolate the Unfathomer from the discordant city.

Bind protects Brassreach quickly and can be humane if carefully made, but it restricts an emergent life that never chose to exist or suffer. Large portions of the old resonance network become inaccessible or inert.

A strong Bind is stable, monitored, and paired with a commitment to repair conditions before reopening contact. A weak Bind is another act of deferred responsibility.

### 14.5 Banish

**Concept:** Sever the resonant pathways through which the Unfathomer reaches Brassreach.

The entity withdraws, disperses, or becomes unreachable in deeper stone and water. The immediate flood subsides, but the city loses much of the living resonance underlying its oldest systems.

Waterworks must be rebuilt through more ordinary mechanical means. Some structures settle or fail. The city survives at the cost of part of what made it extraordinary.

Banish is not framed as slaying an evil being. It is an emergency separation with severe cultural, ecological, and technical consequences.

### 14.6 Hold

**Concept:** Use incomplete preparation to halt the immediate rise and buy time.

Hold is the normal two-Key resolution. It stabilizes selected pressures and creates a temporary calm. It does not reveal or correct the full network.

The epilogue should state what remains missing and give Brassreach a credible period in which another expedition or civic effort can continue the work.

Hold is a valid ending, not a failure screen.

### 14.7 Ending quality matrix

| Preparation | Likely quality |
|---|---|
| Three Keys, strong repairs, full record, broad cooperation | Stable Concord or strongest version of another chosen ending |
| Three Keys, incomplete alliances or evidence | Effective but politically or structurally compromised resolution |
| Two Keys and strong supporting work | Credible Hold or costly version of Bind, Channel, or Banish |
| Two Keys and weak preparation | Shorter reprieve, major damage, and unresolved public conflict |
| One or no Keys | The Gate cannot perform a city-scale intervention; the expedition may end in evacuation, retreat, or personal tragedy rather than an Unfathomer resolution |

### 14.8 No final coin flip

The final decision should not succeed or fail because of one isolated random roll.

Rolls during Weight, Tone, and Pattern determine casualties, damage, resource loss, available evidence, and ending quality. The living Choice commits the course the player selected.

## 15. Encounters, equipment, and progression

### 15.1 Encounter purpose

Every major encounter should do at least two of the following:

- Advance the route.
- Reveal how Brassreach functions.
- Show a consequence of neglect.
- Develop an ally or institution.
- Demonstrate the Unfathomer indirectly.
- Create or repair a harmonic anchor.
- Test a skill or item in a context the player can understand.

Filler combat and arbitrary traps should be avoided.

### 15.2 Conflict types

The campaign should mix:

- Environmental danger.
- Failing machinery.
- Displaced animals.
- Misaligned automata or safety systems.
- Institutional obstruction.
- Dialogue and testimony.
- Resource decisions.
- Limited physical combat when avoidance or de-escalation is also plausible.

The Unfathomer does not spawn evil minions. Creatures and mechanisms become dangerous because their environment is disturbed.

### 15.3 Fail-forward structure

Failure redirects the situation rather than repeating the same paragraph or blocking the campaign.

A failed check may cause:

- Injury.
- Lost time or supplies.
- A damaged route.
- Reduced trust.
- A weaker harmonic anchor.
- A clue learned at greater cost.
- A later ending complication.

The player should understand what changed.

### 15.4 Lost Encounter recovery

Gold-funded and item-funded rerolls are primarily game abstractions. When narrated, they should receive practical explanations:

- Gold secures emergency labor, treatment, replacement materials, or a second attempt before access closes.
- An item is consumed, broken, abandoned, or traded to create another opportunity.

Keys, irreplaceable records, equipped essentials, and major relics should remain protected unless a specific authored choice clearly warns otherwise.

### 15.5 Items

Every authored reward must explain:

- What the item is.
- Where it came from.
- Why the player receives it.
- What it can plausibly help with.

Ordinary supplies may be purchased or salvaged. Significant equipment should connect to a person, place, craft, or decision.

The Keys remain separate from ordinary loot and economy systems.

### 15.6 Character statistics

STR, DEX, INT, and CHA are game abstractions with clear fictional meanings:

- **STR:** Force, endurance, bracing, carrying, and direct physical control.
- **DEX:** Balance, timing, precision, stealth, and safe movement.
- **INT:** Observation, technical reasoning, lore, diagnosis, and pattern recognition.
- **CHA:** Trust, mediation, leadership, testimony, and clear expression of intent.

Equipment bonuses should arise from concrete usefulness. A carried or equipped item should never improve a roll merely because its rarity is high.

### 15.7 Economy

Gold represents coin, scrip, and readily tradable value abstracted into one resource. Merchants exist because official supply chains are strained and the player operates across institutional boundaries.

Dorrin represents sanctioned Warden supply. Sella represents salvage and practical exchange in areas where formal logistics have failed.

## 16. Narrative standards

### 16.1 Voice

- Present tense.
- Second person for player experience.
- Concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Regal and dramatic without obscuring action.
- Rich sensory detail anchored to state, danger, or discovery.

### 16.2 Beat length

Routine actions may use 40–100 words. Major discoveries, act transitions, and explanations may use 120–300 words when the additional space makes cause and consequence clearer.

Longer prose is welcome when it answers meaningful questions. Length should not be used to repeat atmosphere without advancing understanding.

### 16.3 Exposition rule

Every abstract claim should connect to observable evidence.

Weak:

> The city has fallen out of harmony because its values declined.

Strong:

> Lithen places two repair orders side by side. The older order specifies bell-metal, a three-day cooling period, and a cistern crew empowered to stop the work. The newer order substitutes brittle brass, cuts the cooling time in half, and charges any delay against the workers' pay. The low tremor in the table begins where the newer repair entered service.

### 16.4 Action clarity

Every scene should make clear:

- Where the player is.
- Who is present.
- What is happening now.
- What caused the immediate problem, if known.
- What danger or opportunity exists.
- What the available choices mean.
- What changed after the choice.

### 16.5 Mystery without confusion

The cause may be unknown while the immediate action remains clear. The player can understand that water is rising, a bell is producing an extra overtone, and a wall will fail without yet knowing why those events are connected.

Mystery should come from missing explanation, not vague description.

### 16.6 The Unfathomer on the page

Use water-like, neural, luminous, pressure-based, and continuous imagery.

Good motifs include:

- A current responding across distant pools at once.
- Cerulean or teal light moving through stone seams.
- Suspended particles aligning like thought passing through water.
- Pressure releasing as repaired tones settle.
- A vast response arriving everywhere without traveling visibly between points.

Avoid:

- Spoken all-capital questions.
- Taunts, riddles, or eloquent negotiations.
- A face, conventional limbs, or a monster silhouette unless explicitly presented as a mistaken perception.
- Icy elemental imagery.
- Smoke-only presentation.
- Describing it as a chorus of separate voices.
- Treating it as malicious because it causes harm.

### 16.7 Lithen's exposition

Lithen should identify levels of certainty:

- “This record proves…”
- “The measurements suggest…”
- “My present theory is…”
- “I named it because…”
- “I do not know…”

She may be wrong in detail. She should not be carelessly vague.

### 16.8 Dialogue

- **Lithen:** Clear, exact, patient, and willing to explain evidence.
- **Brunna:** Direct, practical, focused on public safety.
- **Orra:** Economical, decisive, increasingly reflective about the cost of duty.
- **Dorrin:** Material-minded and skeptical of unprepared heroics.
- **Sella:** Alert, dryly humorous, and attentive to what abandoned objects reveal.
- **Piera:** Fast, observant, and comfortable with partial truths.
- **House officials:** Polished, procedural, and skilled at making harmful delay sound responsible.
- **Worksfolk:** Technically specific; never written as an undifferentiated suffering mass.

### 16.9 Choices

Choice labels should describe actions and foreseeable stakes, not hide them behind poetic abstractions.

Good:

> Hold the cracked brace while Orra evacuates the lower platform. (STR)

Weak:

> Accept the burden of Weight.

### 16.10 Repetition and callbacks

Recurring elements should evolve:

- The low overtone first appears as an anomaly, later as evidence, and finally as part of a readable living response.
- A denied surface repair should reappear in Archive records and Depths consequences.
- Worker testimony gathered early should affect later trust or Gate preparation.
- Equipment should solve problems related to its established function.

Do not repeat storylines or descriptive paragraphs verbatim.

## 17. Recommended explanatory passages

These are tone and clarity references, not mandatory final script.

### 17.1 Lithen names the Unfathomer

> Lithen does not begin with a legend. She begins with your ledger. The brass leaf carries the faint overtone you recorded in the Halls, and three older plates answer when she sets it into the reading frame. One came from a cistern collapse thirty-two years ago. Another was impressed during a Brassworks failure before Brunna was born. The third predates the present map of the city.
>
> “These systems were never joined by pipe or gear,” Lithen says. “But something beneath them responds as one body. Not quickly. Not with thought as we understand it. Yet when one part is disturbed, the whole adjusts.”
>
> She turns the frame and the overtone passes through the table. Water trembles in a covered ink cup.
>
> “I have avoided naming it. Names are too easily mistaken for understanding. But we must be able to discuss what we are investigating.” Her hand remains on the frame until the vibration fades. “The records cannot find its boundary. The gauges cannot find its depth. Even its awareness may have no center. For now, I call it the Unfathomer.”

### 17.2 The player produces the first improvement

> The repaired bell does not ring louder. It rings cleanly. Its bronze note enters the wall, passes through the pipe beneath your boots, and returns without the low distortion that followed every earlier strike. For one breath, the cellar water stops climbing.
>
> The crew notices before the officials do. Faces turn toward the gauge. Its needle eases back by the width of a thumbnail.
>
> This is not a cure. The wall remains cracked, the lower rooms remain flooded, and one sound cannot retune a city. But your ledger records the change, the old pipe carries it onward, and somewhere far below, something vast encounters relief where it expected only pain.

### 17.3 The Unfathomer responds at the Gate

> No voice answers. The water does not form a face.
>
> The response arrives everywhere at once. Pressure leaves the chamber walls. Teal light passes through the brass veins without traveling from one vein to the next, as if the whole Gate remembered brightness in the same instant. Through Stone comes the weight of caverns settling. Through Brass comes the repaired bell above, joined by the Silent Works and every tuned anchor along your descent. Through Echo comes the long memory of those sounds before neglect bent them apart.
>
> The presence beneath Brassreach has felt promises before only as noise. What reaches it now is different. The repairs already exist. The witnesses have already spoken. The Keys are already turning together. It cannot know whether the city will keep faith for a century, but it can perceive that the movement has begun.

## 18. Player knowledge and interface language

### 18.1 Opening restrictions

Before meeting Lithen, player-facing text must not present these as established facts:

- The name Unfathomer.
- The entity's nature.
- The Gate as the solution.
- The Three Keys as the campaign objective.
- A known relationship between the floods and living resonance.

The opening may establish Brassreach, Threadbearers, visible inequality, failing public works, and unusual recurring patterns.

### 18.2 After Lithen

The Ledger or codex may add an **Unfathomer** entry clearly labeled as Lithen's working theory. It should become more certain as the player gathers evidence.

### 18.3 The objective system

Objectives should describe immediate work:

- Inspect the flooded residence.
- Compare the three matching records with Lithen.
- Reach Orra's watch and verify the old pressure route.
- Restore the Brassworks tone and secure the Brass Key.

Avoid objectives such as “Answer the Measure” before the player has a concrete mechanism and action to perform.

### 18.4 Ledger categories

Recommended diegetic sections are:

- Current Writ.
- Observations.
- Testimony.
- Routes.
- Institutional support.
- Keys.
- Unresolved questions.
- Consequences.

## 19. Canon glossary

### Brassreach

A dwarven-majority city built through resonant craft above ancient caverns and cisterns. Its social decline and neglected works have fallen into mutually reinforcing discord.

### Living resonance

Life-like organization emerging from sustained relationships among resonance, matter, water, craft, repetition, and intention.

### The Unfathomer

Lithen's name for the immense continuous living resonance beneath Brassreach. It spreads upward in search of the harmony in which it first rested.

### Threadbearer

A graduate of the Threadbearer Institute and chartered civic investigator, cartographer, witness, inspector, and mediator who preserves an unbroken record from cause to consequence.

### Thread Ledger

A tamper-evident field record whose sealed entries leave corresponding patterns in Archive master plates.

### Deep Writ

Authorization allowing a Threadbearer to enter restricted under-works, inspect sealed systems, request cross-office records, and carry Keys under witnessed custody.

### Founding Covenant

Brassreach's constitution, establishing civic stewardship, public record, shared responsibility, and limits upon inherited authority. It is not a pact with the Unfathomer.

### Cadence Law

The combined legal and engineering tradition for making public decisions compatible with structural load, resonant systems, historical precedent, and accountable choice.

### Weight

The Stone Measure: what must be supported, protected, endured, and paid for.

### Tone

The Brass Measure: whether distinct systems can function together in stable relation.

### Pattern

The Echo Measure: what is remembered, repeated, inherited, or concealed.

### Choice

The accountable living decision made after Weight, Tone, and Pattern are understood. It has no Key.

### Stone Key

The Gate instrument governing foundations, load, pressure, and structural consequence.

### Brass Key

The Gate instrument governing tonal calibration, communication, and active resonant machinery.

### Echo Key

The Gate instrument governing stored configurations, historical comparison, and recurrence.

### Gate of Measures

A monumental calibration mechanism, constitutional safeguard, instructional structure, and route into the deepest resonant network.

### The Counter

A non-sentient Gate instrument that compares Key inputs, loads, stored patterns, and recorded responsibility.

### First Register

The earliest integrated account of Brassreach's civic principles, structures, maintenance relationships, and founding practices, later separated among collections.

### High Houses

Hereditary families that dominate Brassreach's government and wealth. They perpetuate systemic neglect without constituting one unified conspiracy.

### Mullinen Wardens

A deep public-works tradition descended from Mullinen the Stout, now divided over the meaning of endurance and duty.

### Brass Choir

A guild and teaching tradition preserving bellfounding, tuning, and part of the founders' resonance craft.

### Halls

Upper civic corridors and inspection works forming the threshold to the old city below.

### Archives

The repository of civic law, testimony, engineering history, Threadbearer records, and the Echo Key.

### Depths

Warden routes, active waterworks, buried workshops, and old infrastructure beneath the city.

### Cistern Fields

The immense reservoir caverns where infrastructure, natural systems, and the Unfathomer most deeply overlap.

### The Tangles

A dense craft and residential district known for unofficial routes, rumor, and practical knowledge.

## 22. Writer and implementation checklist

Before adding or revising a story scene, confirm:

1. Does the player know every term the scene assumes?
2. Is the immediate physical situation clear even if the larger mystery is not?
3. Does the scene show a cause, consequence, or institution rather than relying on atmosphere alone?
4. Does any abstract lore claim have observable evidence?
5. Is the Unfathomer nonverbal, continuous, and non-malicious?
6. Does the scene avoid implying that one repair will redeem the city?
7. If the player helps, does the action create a credible material or social improvement?
8. If an item is awarded, is its provenance and usefulness explained?
9. If a Key is involved, does the scene respect its institutional custody and meaning?
10. Does failure redirect the story with a legible cost?
11. Do choices describe actions rather than abstract themes?
12. Are Lithen's facts, theories, and uncertainties distinguished?
13. Are workers written as knowledgeable individuals rather than scenery?
14. Does a House official affect resources, evidence, access, or the ending if introduced?
15. Does the scene preserve the distinction between the Founding Covenant and the Unfathomer?
16. Does the current route contribute to the chain of harmonic anchors the Gate later connects?
17. Does the finale reflect accumulated preparation instead of one arbitrary roll?
18. Is any new canon labeled and added to this document?

## 23. One-paragraph handoff

Brassreach is a dwarven city built through living resonance, shared stewardship, and craft so harmonious that centuries of it unintentionally gave rise to the Unfathomer: a vast, continuous resonant life distributed through water, stone, and brass beneath the city. The Unfathomer is neither evil nor conventionally intelligent. As hereditary inequality and neglected public works made Brassreach physically discordant, the entity began spreading upward in search of the harmony it remembers, raising the cistern waters with it. The player begins as a probationary Threadbearer, a public investigator and witness whose tamper-evident records connect decisions to consequences. After discovering one low overtone across unrelated failures, the player earns a Deep Writ, works with Lithen the Wise, Orra Vale, Wardens, Worksfolk, and the Brass Choir, and gathers the Stone, Brass, and Echo Keys representing Weight, Tone, and Pattern. At the Gate of Measures—a citywide calibration mechanism, constitutional safeguard, and instruction left by the founders—the player supplies the living Choice. Their journey has already created the first connected repairs and civic cooperation the Unfathomer has felt in generations. The finale cannot restore Brassreach overnight; it can calm, redirect, isolate, banish, or temporarily hold the Unfathomer and give the city time to decide whether it will truly reform.

## 24. Preservation note

Do not flatten Brassreach into generic dwarven fantasy, a morality fable without material causation, or a monster hunt.

Its distinguishing ideas are:

- Craft whose harmony became alive.
- An emergent being suffering inside the civilization that made it.
- Social choices becoming physical infrastructure.
- Public records that preserve the thread from decision to consequence.
- Three institutional Keys and one irreducibly living Choice.
- A constitutional machine built as instruction for the future.
- A protagonist who earns authority by listening, observing, repairing, and recording.
- A best ending that begins difficult work rather than declaring the city healed.

End of Master Lore Bible.
